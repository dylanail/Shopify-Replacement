# Creatives

"It's not the format that makes it work, it's what the ad is saying with the
format." Every ad needs an angle; a format is not an angle.

## Formats

**Video**: voiceover with B-roll; subtitles with B-roll (the two that work
most often); slideshow with text; talking-head UGC; professional video
(rarely converts); animation (awareness only).

**Image**: static graphic design; product photo plus headline; infographic;
native style (never fake press); quote or heavy-text; collage; UGC photo;
meme. Start with images, then video.

## Concept, angle, hook

- **Concept**: the big idea of the test, internal ("test sub-avatars",
  "video version of the winner").
- **Angle**: the customer's reason to buy, external. "Stops tossing and
  turning" is an angle; "us vs them" is not.
- **Hook**: how the angle is said in the first two seconds. Direct beats
  clever until you are advanced: "14+ hours, no swelling".

Angle extraction: desire → the behaviour they currently do to get it → the
gap that remains → the angle. ("Restorative sleep" → "wears nose strips" →
"still tired" → "better than nose strips".)

## The 13 static templates

| # | Template | Pattern |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Avatar callout | "[Avatar], …" with a real review that mentions the same detail |
| 2 | Solution exaggeration | "[Big number] hours, no [problem]" |
| 3 | Borrowed credibility | A familiar mechanism attached to the product |
| 4 | This is not the product | "This is not a [category]. It's a [outcome]." |
| 5 | Specific pain point | "[Named pain] for good" |
| 6 | Accidental outcome | "I accidentally [outcome] at [specific]" |
| 7 | Relatable hook | Product shown, relatable line |
| 8 | Indirect avatar | "[Someone close to them] always [problem]" |
| 9 | Free | Offer-led, product-aware |
| 10 | Classy offer | A clean BOGO or bundle |
| 11 | Simplified us vs them | "This is [before]. This is [after]." |
| 12 | News-style | Real coverage only |
| 13 | Minimal us vs them | "No [x]. No [y]. No [z]." |

Plus two layouts: headline + product + four bullets; the phone-notes
screenshot. Never fake a review, a comment or a press mention.

## Video anatomy

Three hooks, each with its own bridge (hook + bridge ≈ 8 seconds), one
universal hold (objection → claim → proof → benefit → secondary benefit), one
CTA (guarantee and price framing). About 45 seconds. Every line carries a
visual instruction. Hooks are problem-aware; the hold educates toward the
product.

## Copy rules

1. Nobody cares about you or the product; write through their lens.
2. Results only; never argue about copy, test it.
3. Write how people talk; grade 6 reading level.
4. Never too long, only too boring: open loops, no stop before the CTA.
5. Research is 80% of the work.
6. Show, don't tell: "walking past the vending machine at 3pm without
   thinking about it" beats "more energy".
7. Don't categorise yourself ("like X but Y"); own a mechanism.
8. Four frames: new and only; easy and anybody; safe and predictable; big and fast.

## Winner and loser

Loser: under 10% of spend after seven days or below KPI. Winner: 10%+ and
at KPI. Super winner: lets the budget scale at KPI. Nothing is done until
its learnings are written down.

## Content sourcing

Stock B-roll → film it yourself → an in-house creator with direction →
marketplace creators → AI B-roll. Test a script with existing footage
before handing it to a creator. Never rip a creator's face or full video.
