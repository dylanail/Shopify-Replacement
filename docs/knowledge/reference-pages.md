# The reference pages

What the fifteen pages the owner pointed at actually do, read in September
2026 with a crawler that renders the page and returns its text and images.
Each page is broken down in the order a visitor meets it, with the words
kept verbatim where the words are the lesson. `pages.md` is the rule book
that follows from these; this file is the evidence.

Two shapes, and two front doors that can stand in front of either:

| Shape | Pages | Seen at |
|---|---|---|
| **Shopify-style store** | home, collections, product pages with a bundle buy box, cart drawer, checkout | alevia, honex, callixe, beautyfrombees, slidebelts, selenepets, flovir, babybliss |
| **Funnel** | one long sales page or a short offer page, a checkout with a bump, upsell, downsell, thank-you; no navigation | pipitea (sales page + offer page), smoothspine, rosabella, primals |
| Front door: **advertorial** | an article that teaches, then links to the product page or the sales page | celinva "7 reasons" |
| Front door: **quiz** | one question per screen, the result presents the offer | rosabella |
| A landing page that owns its buy box | a store page that is a sales page, every CTA an anchor to `#buy` | celinva LP v1 |

The stores are, without exception, single-product funnels dressed as stores:
Selene's "product page" and Flovir's "home page" are long sales pages with
the buy box at the bottom and ten identical CTAs anchoring to it. Only
SlideBelts is a catalogue.

## The buy box, as it recurs

Alevia, Honex, Callixe, Selene, Babybliss and Celinva share one skeleton,
top to bottom:

1. **Credential or proof eyebrow.** "PT, Chiropractor, & CMT recommended" · "Rated 4.7 | (100,000+) Reviews" · "#1 Vet Recommended Dog Fountain" · "Rated 4.8 / 5.0 | 'Excellent'" → `#reviews`.
2. **Title**, then a **one-sentence mechanism subtitle**: "Activates your mitochondria to support nerve function, ease joint discomfort, and fight inflammation where it starts." · "The 3-in-1 therapy pillow that combines heat, vibration, and gentle traction: just 15 minutes a day eases stiffness…"
3. **Three to eight bold-lead check bullets**, outcome-negations for cold traffic: "No More Colic & Reflux" · "End Flat Head Worries Now" · "Proven to Help Your Dog **Drink 2x More Water**" · "**Mitochondria** – Your cells producing energy again".
4. **Offer label**: "Limited Time Offer" · "Labor Day Sale: $70 OFF + 5 Free Gifts" · "HURRY! Deal ends at 11:59 PM".
5. **Selector guides inside the buy box** when there are options: a size guide by dog weight with "Not sure? Go 7L — more fresh water is always better."; a model guide with the price on each and "MOST POPULAR" on the dearer one.
6. **What's included**, each free item with its crossed-out value: "2x Replacement Filters — $19.98 FREE / Dental Boost — $39.98 FREE / 5-Year Warranty — FREE / 1-Year Money Back Guarantee — FREE / Total: **5 Free Gifts** Included".
7. **Quantity tiers** (see the table below), one pre-selected and badged, the CTA price mirroring the selection.
8. **Pre-checked add-ons**: "AntiSplash Mat - 40% OFF · $17.98 ~~$29.98~~" · "Replacement Filter Pack · SAVE 20%".
9. **Stock and ship-date line**: "✓ In Stock, ready to ship - arrives by September 8th" · "Ships by Fri, Sep 04" · "In stock - limited units available".
10. **The button, with the live price in it**: "ADD TO CART • $59.99 ~~$79.99~~" · "$54.95 • CLAIM OFFER" · "CLAIM MY OFFER →" · "Upgrade My Dog's Health →".
11. **Two or three trust chips directly under the button**, the same ones under every CTA on the page: "5-Year Warranty · 1-Year Guarantee · #1 Vet-Recommended" · "Free shipping to United States / Not sold in stores or on Amazon — only here".
12. **The compliance line after the button**, in soft words: "By default, your selected package will continue to be shipped and billed automatically (every 1 or 2 months) unless you choose to skip, pause, cancel, or select the one-time purchase option below." · "Pause or Cancel anytime · Refills ship every 28 days · … renew at $59.96".
13. **Payment icons** and "Safe & Secure Checkout"; HSA/FSA eligibility where it applies ("Pay using HSA/FSA with …").
14. **A named guarantee** with a shield: "90-Day Money Back Guarantee — Love your results within 90 days or get 100% of your money back, no questions asked."
15. **Accordions**: Description · The Results (a week-by-week timeline) · The guarantee · Shipping & Returns · Specs.
16. **One emotional testimonial** right there: "Love it! The first night using this, he slept 6 hours straight and didn't spit up once. I CRIED TEARS OF RELIEF…" — Grace R., Nashville, USA.

### Urgency seen

Concrete, never generic: a ship-by date ("arrives by September 8th", "Ships by Fri, Sep 04", "Ships in 24h"), a daily deadline ("Deal ends at 11:59 PM"), a flip-clock countdown bar ("FLASH SALE – 50% OFF"), "clearance stock — once it's gone, the 50% price is gone", a cart reservation timer ("Cart reserved for 05:30"), a purchase cap ("Max 10 Units Per Customer"), exclusivity ("Not sold in stores or on Amazon — only here"), and a live counter ("0 Dog Parents bought this in the last 24 hours" — captured at its uninitialised value, which is the lesson: render a real number or nothing).

## What the pages share

**The argument below the fold, in the order it recurs**: problem or agitation (often as image + caption micro-scenes) → the named mechanism with numbers → stages or a timeline → expert or peer-reviewed proof → comparison against categories, never brands → video or photo reviews → origin and manufacturing → the guarantee restated → FAQ of six to eight → the offer again → the sticky bar.

**Named things**: the mechanism (OFFLOAD / ALIGN / HOLD, ThermoTrac™, Living Hinge®, "activating AMPK", "Pediatric-Approved 10° Incline", "the first dental formula engineered to survive carbon filtration"), the material (OrthoFlex™, BreatheEase™), the guarantee (The Empty Bottle Promise, 'Happy Child', 'sit without pain'). A proprietary noun makes the claim ownable; the platform's writers should name the mechanism from the research and use the name everywhere.

**Root-cause reframes that absolve the buyer**: "IT WAS NEVER YOUR CHAIR." · "Why Feeling 'Off' Is Not a Willpower Problem" · "Your expensive Herman Miller didn't fail. It just wasn't designed to fix this specific problem." · "Your dog's breath is not normal — it's a warning."

**Cost stacking**: "$5,930+ … 20x Cheaper" · "14x Cheaper Than Weekly Appointments" · "The bag that pays for itself — $75 saved on your very first round trip" · "$-/day with a 5-year warranty".

**Offer stacking beats a bare discount**: "$70 OFF + 5 Free Gifts" with each gift's value crossed out · "15% OFF + 2 Free Gifts ($94 Value)" · a gift-with-purchase threshold stated three times with its exclusions.

**Proof is layered**: the rating+count eyebrow, three mini quotes in the hero, credentialed named experts (DO, VMD, DVM, PT, "Dr. Andy Miller, 20 years"), press logos and press pull-quotes, static review cards with city or country and "Verified Buyer", then a real review feed with photos, and for "does it work" products, video reviews. Reviews are specific (age, condition, climate, years driving) and echo the page's own hooks.

**Compliance sits after the CTA**: the auto-renew disclosure under the button, "results may vary" under the timeline, the FDA or vet disclaimer and "not part of Facebook" in the footer, the testimonial-compensation line, the class-of-device note under the science. Nothing here is hidden and nothing here is above the fold.

**The cart drawer sells**: a reservation timer, the free-shipping gap, one or two add-ons (a wrap, a warranty extension, shipping protection at $3.99), the guarantee line.

**The image set a page needs** (the photo briefs, confirmed): the hero product, the product per tier (1, 2, 3 packs), the "before" lifestyle micro-scenes, a mechanism diagram or cutaway, the expert's portrait, a factory or origin photo, before/after pairs, a "goes everywhere" lifestyle grid, review photos, the what's-included flat-lay, a texture macro for skincare, infographic gallery slides for mobile.

**What a naive builder ships**: JS-only widgets at their initial state ("0 bought", "$-/day", "00 Day"), template leftovers (foreign alt text, another store's icons, a sister brand's link, `placeholder-image.png`), inconsistent counts (574+ vs 118,802+), two cookie banners, a "Sold out" default in a sticky bar, dead `#` links, typos in the CTA. The health report should flag every one of these.
