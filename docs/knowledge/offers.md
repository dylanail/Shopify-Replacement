# Offers

"Every single winning page that we have ever made has honestly just had a
better offer." An offer moves conversion rate and AOV at once, so ROAS moves
more than any page layout can. Below about $100k/month the offer matters
more than the advertorial, the listicle or the page builder.

## Before any offer: know the numbers

Recalculate gross margin and breakeven ROAS for every offer, separately for
every tier of a stacked offer (see `testing.md`).

## The types, in the order to test them

| Offer | Definition | Use when | Numbers from the course |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Bundle and save** | Group units or complementary items at a discount | Always test first; works without discounting the hero | 2-pack and 3-pack; hero + three gifts "worth $122" free; store credit ($20–30) as the bundle component, ~80% never redeemed |
| **$ off / % off** | Flat reduction to test price points | Early; "some of our best pages were the product with 10–15% off" | Move the welcome-flow 10% onto the page |
| **Buy X get X free** | Extra units free | Products people use more than one of; low AOV → raise the minimum quantity | Minimum 5: buy 2 get 3 free; 3 for the price of 2 scaled a brand to $1M/month |
| **Buy X get X% off** | Discount on the next unit | When margin cannot afford free | Buy one get one 50% off; site-wide 30% plus 50% off the second |
| **Free gift** | A no-cost item makes a bundle | Any time, on top of the main offer | Ebook, complementary item, mystery gift |
| **Free shipping** | Unconditional or above a threshold | Never the core offer; test it | Charge under ~$5 otherwise; threshold at $100 lifts AOV |
| **Store credit** | Credit or gift card by email | Single-SKU stores; advanced | $20–30 |

Wrappers that ride on any offer: a timer with "ending soon", a 30-day
money-back guarantee, a "limited offer" badge on the buy box, combined social
proof across the brand ("4.85 from 10,000+ customers" only when true).

## Phrasing: the law of large numbers

Show whichever number is bigger. $20 off a $400 item beats "5% off"; "30%
off" a $30 item beats "$9 off". Low AOV → percentage; high AOV → dollars.
Anchors must be believable: a $130 anchor on a $30 sale price is not.

## The RatVac case (one offer, 2x spend, 3x ROAS)

Same ads, same spend, new page: 1.18x → 3.16x in a week, then spend doubled
at 3.59x. The page: "Save $50 — ending soon" above the fold with a timer;
trust bar; combined proof; problem headline showing the alternatives failing;
authority positioning; story of each failed alternative and its cost; product
reveal with proof-of-work; how it works in bullets beside the video; more
proof; buy box with the same photo, badge, was/now, guarantee; deeper
education; FAQ; reviews. Price $249 → $199 cost two points of margin and
returned six times the revenue per session.

## Measure revenue per session, not conversion alone

Revenue per session = AOV × conversion rate, compared against CPC. $249 ×
0.42% = $1.04 against a $1.29 CPC loses; $222 × 3.09% = $6.87 against $1.35
wins. The platform reports this per page version and per funnel step.

## Where to build offers by stage

- $1k–10k/month: change the offer on the product page itself.
- $20–30k/month: one good offer plus a template static ad gets here.
- $30–40k/month: dedicated offer pages tested inside the ad platform.
- Under $100k/month: the offer over the advertorial.
