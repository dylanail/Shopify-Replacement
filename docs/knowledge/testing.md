# Numbers, testing and scaling

## Gross margin and the two ROAS lines

- **Gross margin** = (price + shipping charged − COGS − supplier shipping − ~3% processing) ÷ (price + shipping charged). Exclude editors, software, salaries. Overestimate costs.
- **Breakeven ROAS** = 1 ÷ gross margin, rounded up. 67% → 1.5; 55% → 1.82.
- **Target ROAS** = breakeven + 1. Above target: scale. Between: safe, hold. Below breakeven: scale down.
- **Profit from ads** = spend × ROAS × margin − spend. More spend at a lower multiple can out-earn less spend at a higher one, until it does not: $5k at 1.8 earns less than $3k at 2.2.
- **When margin changes** (a new offer): keep the same delta above breakeven. Old 2.0 − 1.43 = 0.57; new floor 1.82 + 0.57 = 2.39.
- Recalculate profit weekly against the bank account, not daily against the dashboard.

## Minimum daily spend

The amount spent regardless of performance, because at zero spend the chance
of finding a winning ad is zero. $50–100/day for most; $150–200 once
established; committed for 30 days.

## Decision windows

- Never act on one day. Three days minimum; seven at ~$100/day.
- Read 7-day click attribution; scale only when 60%+ of purchases are click-based.
- Scale up 20% (5–15% at very large budgets; 1.5–2x when 50–100% above target). Expect ROAS to drop 5–25% per scale.
- Scale down 20% minimum, never below minimum daily spend.

## Definitions

- **3:2:2** — 3 creatives × 2 primary texts × 2 headlines, the unit of a test.
- **Concept** — the big idea of a batch (test sub-avatars, test superlatives, iterate a winner).
- **Angle** — the main reason to buy. Every ad needs one; a format ("us vs them") is not an angle.
- **Variation** — a way of communicating the angle.
- **Format** — image, video, carousel, GIF; then UGC, voiceover, slideshow, product shot.
- Hierarchy: concept → angle → variations → format.

## The three methods

| Method | Shape | Use for |
|---|---|---|
| **Marksman** | Three angles, one execution each | Images, to find direction |
| **Sniper** | One angle, three executions | All video; iterations of a winning image |
| **Shotgun** | Volume without strategy | Only raw creator/UGC content in its own campaign |

One concept per day minimum (three ads); micro-iterations of hooks do not
count as concepts.

## Why bad ads get spend

The platform optimises for volume of conversions at the lowest cost, so the
top spender is usually the best prospecting ad even at a lower ROAS, and the
small-spend high-ROAS ad is usually retargeting its traffic. Before turning
anything off: "if this spend went to the other ads, would the ROAS be
better?" Ninety-five percent of the time, no. Near-zero spend in a CBO means
a bad ad; do not force budget on it. Give a new spend-grabber 2–3 days.

## The 14 questions for a video ad

1. Do the first two seconds hook the right person and keep them?
2. Would the customer want to watch this if it appeared in their feed?
3. Does the copy paint a concrete picture?
4. Does it feel native to the platform?
5. Does it label who the person is?
6. Is it relatable, specifically?
7. Would it make someone smile?
8. Does it look like an ad? (it should not)
9. Gut check: authentic?
10. Which learnings from previous winners are applied?
11. Does it educate toward the core desire in a creative way?
12. Does it tell a story the customer finds interesting?
13. Is the claim escalated playfully and clearly?
14. Does it separate the brand from the competition?

Plus: which awareness level is this ad for?

## Scaling SOP

1. Is the campaign at target?
2. Yes, 48–72 hours consistently, 60%+ click-based → +20% every 24 hours. A limited-time promo goes straight to scaling.
3. No: below breakeven 24–48 hours → −20% (not below minimum). Below target but above breakeven for 48–72 hours → −20%.
4. At minimum and still below: act outside the account — new concepts, funnel bug check, feedback loops, new avatars and mechanisms, study winning ads, test a new offer.

## Feedback loops

List what keeps failing and what keeps working; write hypotheses using the 14
questions; rank by confidence; write the action items; run them; loop. Stay
inside the same system (a failed creative means a better creative). Keep every
loop in one place so a model can turn them into a to-do list.
