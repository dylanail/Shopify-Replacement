# Desires

"A winning product is a product that satisfies a mass desire." Nothing sells
that does not connect to a desire that already exists; marketing channels a
desire onto a product, it never creates one.

## The permanent forces

**Six mass instincts**, ranked by how strongly they move people:

1. Health — survival, energy, pain, appearance of vitality
2. Status — being seen as more, envy, admiration
3. Sex — attraction, romance, being desired
4. Comfort — ease, less effort, less friction
5. Control — certainty, safety, structure, mastery
6. Belonging — family, tribe, identity with a group

**Six technology problems** that products get bought to solve: time, money,
effort, physical pain, mental pain and complexity. A product that saves one
of these for a strong instinct is a candidate; one that saves a weak amount of
one of them for a weak instinct (desk organisers, cable clips) only works on
organic reach.

## The forces of change

- **Style trends** — the fashion of the moment inside a market.
- **Mass education** — what the market has just been taught by media, a
  documentary, a viral post, a new ingredient.

Evaluate a desire or trend on three axes before building on it:

| Axis | Question |
|---|---|
| Scope | How many people feel this? |
| Urgency | How badly, right now? |
| Staying power | Will it still be felt in a year? |

Flat or rising on Google Trends (US, 5 years) passes; consistently declining
fails; a single viral spike followed by a decline fails.

## Elevation and channelling

- **Elevation**: take a small desire and connect it to a bigger one. A
  water bottle is hydration (weak) until it is emotional support and status
  (strong).
- **Channelling**: a headline combines an instinct with a trend or a piece of
  education. "Doctors are finally talking about X" (education + health).
  "The bag every stylist in Paris carries" (style trend + status).
- **Valence and intensity**: a desire can be approached through hope or
  through fear. Fear is stronger and short-lived; hope compounds. Never lie
  about either; the platform never invents the fear.

## Desire hunting

Where the customer's own language lives, in order of usefulness:

1. Reddit threads and forums for the problem (not the product)
2. Amazon reviews of the competitors, 1–3 stars first, then 5 stars
3. YouTube comments under review and how-to videos
4. TikTok comments under the top videos for the product
5. AnswerThePublic and autocomplete for the questions people type

Collect **customer language snippets** verbatim; they become hooks,
headlines, FAQ questions and review prompts. Collect **ad ideas** as you go,
each tied to the snippet that produced it.

The deep-research prompt: give the model a product overview document (what it
is, what it does, who buys it, what it costs, what it replaces) and ask for the
desires ranked, the language snippets, the objections, the competitors and the
angles, with the instruction to say when it does not know.

## The desire calendar

Which desires peak when, used to explain a bad month and to choose messaging,
not to replace the rest of the work.

| Month | Theme | Lead desires | Angles | Lead time |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| January | Fresh start | Control, health, status | Transformation, organisation, financial discipline; health spend up 35–40% | Seed in December |
| February | Belonging | Belonging | Romance, self-appreciation, friends and coworkers | Messaging from mid-January |
| March–April | Action and renewal | — | Spring reset, outdoors, visible change | — |
| May | Appreciation | Belonging | Mothers, graduation, memorial | Late April |
| June | Achievement and paternal | Status | Fathers, summer readiness | Late May |
| July–August | Summer, back-to-school prep | Comfort, control | 67% of back-to-school shoppers start by early July; late August tightens | Early July |
| September | Achievement and structure | Control, status, belonging | Routine, learning, "September is the new January", parental success | Late August |
| October | Halloween | Status, belonging, control | Identity expression, gatherings, home transformation, safe scares | Late September |
| November | Gratitude and preparation | Belonging, comfort, control | Togetherness, looking your best, value, list-building | Late October |
| December | Connection and celebration | Belonging | Gifting, reflection; peak emotional spend | Mid-November |

Rule: people buy *for* the occasion, not *on* it. Message a lead time ahead so
orders ship in time.
