# Awareness, sophistication and the three resets

## Awareness (how much the buyer already knows)

Unaware → problem-aware → solution-aware → product-aware → most-aware.

Beginners target **solution-aware or product-aware** buyers: people who know
the category exists and are choosing within it. Unaware traffic needs far more
skill and longer pages. Product-aware ads ("ours is lab-tested for more X",
"easier to clean than the one you were about to buy") scale to large budgets
on their own.

## Sophistication (how many claims the market has already heard)

| Stage | What the market has heard | What works |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Nothing | A plain claim: "lose weight" |
| 2 | The claim | A bigger claim: "lose 10 lb in 10 days" |
| 3 | Bigger claims | A **mechanism**: how it works, why this one works when the others did not |
| 4 | Mechanisms | A bigger, better, faster mechanism |
| 5 | Everything | **Identity**: the product as who the buyer is, plus proof and experience |

Most paid-social markets are at 3–5. The response is never to shout louder; it
is one of three resets that put the market back at stage 1 for this product.

## Reset 1: new mechanism

Name the reason this works when the others did not. It can be real and
already in the product (double-walled insulation, hydrolysed collagen, the
fit that hides a gut and widens shoulders) or a re-framing of a feature
nobody had named. Examples from the course: shilajit resin → gummies; a
hoodie cut 10–20% longer; "the bag drawer" on a self-cleaning litter box.

The mechanism must be true, must be something the supplier can replicate, and
must be shown, not asserted: a cutaway, a demo, a before/after, a comparison
row.

## Reset 2: new information

Teach the market something it did not know that makes the product the obvious
answer. "Rats teach each other which traps to avoid, so your $5 snap trap is
a feeding station." "Most collagen is not hydrolysed, so most of it is not
absorbed." The information has to be checkable; the platform never invents
a study, a statistic or an expert.

## Reset 3: new identity (the underserved avatar)

Take the same product to a person the market has not been talking to.
Stanley: construction workers → young women. Jump starter: mechanics → women
who want "easy to use". Fleece leggings → people with leg tattoos. The
product can be unchanged; the page, the imagery and the ads change entirely.

## The rule before testing

"Either you innovate on a product or you innovate on the avatar." If neither
a new mechanism nor an underserved avatar can be named before a product is
tested, do not test it. The platform asks for both in the market analysis
and shows a warning when both are empty.
